# Template Contract Clause Risk Table

This table is based on the reusable clauses of the template contract, not the apartment-specific blanks.

| Clause / Topic | What It Means | Buyer Risk | What To Ask To Change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1.3 Completion timing | Target completion is stated, but timing can move based on outside factors and state acceptance timing. | Medium | Ask for clearer outer deadline and a narrower extension mechanism. |
| 1.4 Transfer timing | Transfer may happen within 6 months after cadastral documents are obtained. | Medium | Ask for a shorter handover window after commissioning and cadastral readiness. |
| 1.5 Assignment restrictions | Buyer cannot freely assign rights or substitute parties without developer consent. | Medium | Ask for a reasonable right to assign, especially in mortgage or family situations. |
| 1.6 Area increase | If final area is larger, buyer must pay extra within 30 days based on contract price per m2. | Medium | Ask for a clear cap or tolerance band before additional payment is due. |
| 1.7 Area decrease | If final area is smaller, developer refunds the difference within 30 days after a written request. | Low | Keep, but ask for automatic refund without a separate written request. |
| 3.1.3 Acceptance / notarial signing | Buyer must accept and sign notarized sale contract within 15 days after notice. | High | Ask for a longer deadline and clearer notice delivery proof. |
| 3.1.3 Storage costs | If buyer delays, apartment may go into responsible storage and fees accrue. | High | Reduce or remove storage fees; ask for a lower cap. |
| 3.1.4 Defects at acceptance | Buyer must record defects at acceptance or later claims weaken. | High | Add a hidden-defects protection clause. |
| 3.1.5 Post-handover costs | Buyer bears title registration, utilities, common-area, and management-related costs. | Medium | Ask for a detailed cost list and when exactly costs start. |
| 3.1.6 / 3.1.7 Alterations | Unauthorized changes trigger fines and restoration obligations. | Medium | Ask for a consent process with response deadlines, not broad discretion. |
| 3.1.8 Contact changes | Buyer must notify changes in details within 7 days. | Medium | Keep, but ensure communication methods are realistic. |
| 3.1.13 Mortgage guarantee reimbursement | If developer pays bank as guarantor, buyer must reimburse all related sums quickly. | High | Cap reimbursement items, extend payment period, require documentary proof. |
| 3.2.4 Right to withhold transfer | Developer may suspend transfer until all buyer obligations and penalties are satisfied. | High | Limit this right to material payment defaults only. |
| 3.2.5 Timing changes by additional agreement | If buyer does not respond within 10 days, timing change may be treated as accepted. | High | Remove automatic acceptance. |
| 3.2.6.3 Repeated mortgage breaches | Two or more mortgage breaches may allow unilateral out-of-court termination. | High | Ask for cure periods and narrower trigger events. |
| 4.1 Payment timing | Buyer must pay 100% within 1 month from signing. | High | Request staged payment or bank-aligned deadline. |
| 4.6 Use of funds | Buyer gives developer broad powers to use and manage the paid funds. | Medium | Ask for clearer use-of-funds boundaries and reporting. |
| 4.7 Fixed price rule | Contract price is fixed except area adjustment. | Low | Keep, but ensure no hidden re-pricing mechanism exists elsewhere. |
| 5.3 Warranty | Warranty is 24 months and only for listed works, with many exclusions. | High | Expand coverage and clarify hidden defects and common systems. |
| 6.1 Buyer late payment penalty | 0.1% per day, capped at 10% of contract sum. | High | Reduce rate and cap. |
| 6.2 Developer unilateral termination | Developer may terminate out of court after buyer-side payment breach. | High | Ask for longer cure period and mandatory written default notice. |
| 6.2 Refund deductions | On buyer-side termination, developer may deduct 10% and other charges. | High | Ask for one closed list of deductions with one aggregate cap. |
| 6.3 Contract deemed not concluded | Payment-stage violation may cause contract to be treated as not concluded. | High | Delete or clarify this clause; it is too aggressive and uncertain. |
| 6.4 Developer late handover penalty | 0.01% per day, capped at 2% of amount actually paid. | High | Increase rate and cap. |
| 6.5 Delay in acceptance | Buyer may owe 2% fine plus losses for failing to accept/sign in time. | High | Reduce fine and require documented actual loss. |
| 6.7 - 6.9 Confidentiality | Broad confidentiality obligations continue for 3 years after termination. | Low | Usually acceptable, but make sure standard disclosures remain allowed. |
| 6.10 Unauthorized replanning | 5% fine plus restoration and damages. | Medium | Keep structure, but narrow the clause to material violations. |
| 7 Force majeure | Delays are excused during force majeure; after 3 months either side may terminate. | Medium | Ask for clearer definition and proof standard. |
| 8 Termination mechanics | Termination can happen by mutual agreement, unilateral developer action, or force majeure. | High | Ask for balanced buyer termination rights. |
| 9.2 Minimum retained expenses | Buyer acknowledges developer may retain expenses, at not less than 5% of contract sum. | High | Clarify whether this stacks with 10% penalty; ideally remove the minimum. |
| 10 Dispute venue | Disputes go to court at property location; proceeding language is Uzbek. | Medium | Ensure buyer understands litigation language and location burden. |
| 12.2 Notice by SMS / messenger / email | Notices are treated as received when sent to listed contact details. | High | Require actual delivery or documented receipt for key notices. |
| Annex 1 Scope of works | Delivery scope is limited to listed items only. | Medium | Make sure the final contract and annex match what is actually promised. |
| Annex 4 Refund procedure | Refund timing is up to 3 months and subject to deductions. | High | Reduce refund period and narrow deduction basis. |

## Summary

The biggest buyer-side risks are concentrated in:
- payment timing;
- unilateral termination;
- mortgage guarantee recourse;
- refund deductions;
- short acceptance deadlines;
- weak developer delay liability;
- notice rules that treat sent messages as received.
