# DDU Template Practical Checklist

This note is based on the template contract only. It ignores placeholder apartment-specific fields that will be filled later and focuses on the reusable legal and commercial terms.

## What This Template Means In Practice

This is a developer-protective shared-construction contract.

The main commercial pattern is:
- the buyer pays fast and in full;
- the developer keeps broad control over handover timing and remedies;
- the buyer carries significant penalty, acceptance, and mortgage-guarantee risk;
- refund rights are limited and subject to deductions;
- developer liability for delay is capped low.

## Terms To Check Carefully In The Final Contract

### 1. Payment Structure
- The template requires 100% payment within 1 month from signing.
- Check whether the final contract still requires full payment in 1 month.
- If mortgage financing is used, confirm whether the bank disbursement timeline fits this deadline.
- Confirm whether any grace period exists.

### 2. Completion And Handover Timing
- Construction is targeted for 2026 Q4 and no later than 2027 Q1 in the template.
- The developer can shift timing if there are external factors or regulatory issues.
- If the developer sends a supplement changing deadlines and you do not object within 10 days, it may be treated as accepted.
- After cadastral documents are received, the developer has up to 6 months to transfer the apartment.

### 3. Area Adjustment
- Final area is provisional until cadastral measurement.
- If area increases, the buyer must pay for the extra area within 30 days.
- If area decreases, the buyer can request a refund within 30 days.
- Check that the final contract clearly states the exact price per m2 used for this adjustment.

### 4. Acceptance Risk
- After the developer notifies readiness, the buyer must sign a notarized sale-purchase agreement within 15 days unless another deadline is written in the notice.
- If the buyer delays without documented valid reason, the apartment may be treated as handed over.
- The developer may place the apartment into storage for up to 2 months.
- Storage costs can run at 0.1% of total contract value per day, capped at 50% of contract value.

### 5. Quality And Warranty
- The warranty period is 24 months.
- Warranty applies only to the listed works in Annex 1.
- Many exclusions apply, including normal wear, misuse, post-handover alterations, third-party damage, and utility incidents.
- At handover, defects must be recorded in the acceptance documents. If not recorded, later claims become much weaker.

### 6. Penalties On The Buyer
- Late payment penalty: 0.1% per day, capped at 10% of contract sum.
- Delay in acceptance or notarized sale contract signing: 2% fine plus related losses.
- Unauthorized alterations / replanning: 5% fine plus restoration costs.
- Breach of confidentiality: 5% fine plus damages.

### 7. Developer Liability
- Late handover penalty from the developer: 0.01% per day.
- Total developer penalty exposure is capped at 2% of the amount actually paid by the buyer.
- This is materially weaker than the buyer-side penalty structure.

### 8. Mortgage / Guarantee Risk
- If the buyer uses mortgage funds and the developer acts as guarantor before title registration, the buyer still remains fully liable to the bank.
- If the developer pays the bank under the guarantee, the buyer must reimburse all resulting costs.
- This can include principal, interest, penalties, commissions, and other losses.
- Reimbursement deadline in the template is 5 calendar days.
- If the buyer breaches the mortgage contract 2 or more times and that causes the developer to pay as guarantor, the developer may terminate the contract out of court.

### 9. Refund Risk On Termination
- If the contract is terminated for buyer-side reasons, money is not simply returned in full.
- The developer can deduct:
- 10% penalty;
- accrued unpaid penalty;
- sums paid by the developer to the bank under guarantee obligations;
- other documented costs;
- plus the template separately states the buyer agrees the developer may retain expenses and that this amount is not less than 5% of contract sum.
- These overlapping deduction clauses should be clarified in the real contract.

### 10. Notices And Communication
- Notices may be treated as received if sent by post, SMS, Telegram, WhatsApp, or email to the contract details.
- Make sure your phone number, email, and address are correct before signing.
- Update clauses require changes to be reported within 7 days.

## Clauses Worth Negotiating If Possible

### Strongest items to push back on
- 100% payment within 1 month.
- 0.1% daily buyer late-payment penalty.
- 10% termination deduction.
- Storage fee of 0.1% per day up to 50% of contract sum.
- Developer delay penalty capped at only 2%.
- Out-of-court unilateral termination rights for the developer.
- Automatic acceptance of deadline extension supplements if no response in 10 days.
- Broad mortgage-guarantee reimbursement language.

### Better buyer protections to request
- clear staged payment schedule instead of full payment in 1 month;
- longer acceptance/signing period after notice;
- lower storage fee or removal of storage fee clause;
- higher developer late-delivery penalty;
- narrower deduction rights on termination;
- clearer cap on all deductions together;
- express obligation to provide documentary proof for every deduction;
- longer response period for additional agreements;
- clarification that messages are deemed received only if actually delivered.

## Handover Checklist

Before signing the acceptance documents, check:
- window frames and glazing;
- entrance door and interior doors;
- locks and fittings;
- wall and floor finish condition;
- bathroom tile condition;
- visible cracks;
- electricity meter and wiring points;
- water and sewer risers;
- internet fiber presence;
- exact room and total area from measurement;
- any mismatch from Annex 1 works list.

Record every issue in writing in the acceptance documents.

## Mortgage Checklist

Before signing the real contract, confirm with the bank and developer:
- whether the developer is acting as guarantor;
- when the guarantee starts and ends;
- what events trigger developer payment to the bank;
- how quickly you must reimburse the developer;
- whether deductions can be taken from amounts already paid;
- what happens if the bank delays disbursement but the 1-month payment deadline still runs;
- whether there is any cure period before termination.

## Questions To Ask Before Signing The Real Version
- What exact event starts the 15-day deadline for notarized sale-purchase signing?
- What proof will the developer provide for readiness and notice delivery?
- Can the 100% payment deadline be aligned with bank disbursement timing?
- Can the termination deduction be limited to one fixed percentage only?
- Can the developer clarify whether the 10% penalty and the separate minimum 5% expense retention can both be charged?
- Can the storage fee clause be reduced or removed?
- Can the buyer get a longer period to inspect and accept the apartment?
- Can the late-delivery penalty be increased?
- Can the mortgage-guarantee reimbursement clause be capped or narrowed?

## Bottom Line

Even as a template, this document is useful because it shows the default risk allocation.

The key point is not the apartment details. The key point is that the template heavily favors the developer on:
- payment timing;
- penalties;
- termination;
- refund deductions;
- mortgage default consequences;
- notice mechanics.

When the final apartment details are inserted, the most important work is to test those numbers against these non-apartment clauses, because those clauses will control the real economic risk.
