# Template Contract Red Flags

This is a short risk summary based on the template contract only. It focuses on the clauses that matter even before apartment-specific details are inserted.

## Highest-Risk Clauses

### 1. Full payment within 1 month
- The template says the buyer must pay 100% within 1 month from signing.
- This is aggressive even without mortgage timing complications.
- If bank disbursement is delayed, the buyer still appears exposed.

### 2. Buyer delay penalties are much stronger than developer delay penalties
- Buyer late payment: 0.1% per day, up to 10% of total contract sum.
- Developer late handover: 0.01% per day, capped at 2% of the amount actually paid.
- The economic pressure is clearly one-sided.

### 3. Developer can terminate out of court
- If payment deadlines are breached for more than 10 days, the developer may terminate unilaterally without court involvement.
- Mortgage-related repeated breaches can also trigger unilateral termination.

### 4. Refunds are not clean refunds
- On buyer-side termination scenarios, the developer can deduct:
- 10% penalty;
- unpaid penalties;
- sums paid to the bank under any guarantee arrangement;
- other documented costs.
- The template also separately says retained expenses may not be less than 5% of contract sum.
- This overlap needs clarification in the final version.

### 5. Mortgage-guarantee risk is severe
- If the developer acts as guarantor before title registration, the buyer still remains fully liable to the bank.
- If the developer pays the bank, the buyer must reimburse principal, interest, penalties, commissions, and other losses.
- Reimbursement period is only 5 calendar days.

### 6. Acceptance deadline is short and costly to miss
- After readiness notice, the buyer must sign the notarized sale-purchase agreement within 15 days.
- If delayed without documented valid reason, the apartment may be treated as transferred.
- The apartment may then be placed into storage.

### 7. Storage charges are very heavy
- Storage can be charged at 0.1% of total contract sum per day.
- Cap is 50% of total contract sum.
- That is an unusually strong financial sanction.

### 8. Automatic acceptance of schedule changes
- If the developer sends an additional agreement changing timing and the buyer does not sign or object within 10 days, it may be treated as accepted.
- This is risky if notices are missed.

### 9. Notice mechanics favor the developer
- Messages sent by post, SMS, Telegram, WhatsApp, or email can be treated as received once sent.
- That makes contact details critically important.

### 10. Warranty is limited
- Warranty period is 24 months.
- It applies only to listed works in Annex 1.
- There are many exclusions.
- If defects are not documented at acceptance, later claims are weaker.

## Clauses That Matter Most In The Final Contract
- Payment deadline and whether it matches mortgage timing.
- Exact price per m2 and final area adjustment method.
- Handover timing and what counts as valid notice.
- Deduction rules if the contract is terminated.
- Mortgage guarantee reimbursement mechanics.
- Storage charge clause.
- Developer delay penalty cap.

## Practical Bottom Line
- The template is not neutral.
- It gives the developer stronger remedies than the buyer.
- The apartment details matter, but the larger risk comes from the reusable framework: payment, penalties, termination, mortgage recourse, and refund deductions.
